# R-31 — Owner brief (2026-10-31), stored verbatim

Dot.Forms — Complete Design Transformation & World-Class Form Creation Experience
1. The Vision
Let's revisit Dot.Forms and fundamentally rethink its design, user experience and form creation journey.
The current design still feels AI-generated. It lacks a distinctive personality, visual character and the level of refinement expected from a world-class product.
I do not want another generic SaaS dashboard with predictable cards, gradients, oversized headings, excessive whitespace and repetitive layouts. I want Dot.Forms to feel like a carefully designed, human-created product that people genuinely enjoy using.
Our ambition is to make Dot.Forms one of the most beautiful, intuitive, engaging and capable form creation platforms in the world.
The experience should make users enjoy creating forms for their businesses. Creating a form should feel more like designing something meaningful than configuring a complicated application.
Study the best form-building experiences available today, understand what makes them enjoyable and identify opportunities to create something substantially better. Do not simply copy competitors. Establish a distinctive Dot.Forms identity.
Consider the experiences offered by Typeform, Tally, Jotform, Fillout, Paperform, Microsoft Forms and other leading platforms.
Our goal is to outperform them through design quality, usability, creative freedom, functionality and the overall experience.
2. Start With Design Research and an Honest Assessment
Before making changes:

1. Inspect the entire existing Dot.Forms application.
2. Review every page, screen, modal, editor, form-building interaction and responsive layout.
3. Identify why the current interface feels AI-generated.
4. Identify repetitive layouts, generic components, poor visual hierarchy, unnecessary complexity and frustrating interactions.
5. Examine all installed design, product design, UI/UX, branding, typography, visual storytelling, interaction design, motion design, accessibility and frontend engineering skills.
6. Read and apply the relevant installed skills. Do not rely on a single design skill.
7. Study leading form-building products and identify the interaction patterns and design decisions that make their experiences successful.

Create a design direction based on actual findings.
Do not begin by simply changing colours, border radiuses or typography. Rethink how the product feels, how users interact with it and how the experience flows from beginning to end.
3. Establish a Distinctive Design Identity
Dot.Forms must have its own recognisable visual identity.
The design should feel:

* Human, warm and approachable.
* Beautiful without being decorative for the sake of decoration.
* Modern, sophisticated and premium.
* Creative and expressive.
* Calm and easy to understand.
* Professional enough for large organisations.
* Intuitive enough for someone creating their first form.

Take inspiration from the craftsmanship of Apple, the clarity of Linear, the simplicity of Tally and the engaging interactions of Typeform, while creating something uniquely ours.
Eliminate AI-generated design patterns
Actively avoid:

* Generic AI-generated dashboard layouts.
* Excessive gradients and glassmorphism.
* Unnecessary floating cards and containers.
* Repetitive rounded rectangles.
* Oversized headings that consume valuable screen space.
* Random decorative icons.
* Unnecessary empty space.
* Inconsistent typography and spacing.
* Unnecessary animations.
* Interfaces where every element competes for attention.

Use intentional typography, carefully considered spacing, strong visual hierarchy, beautiful composition and consistent design language.
Every component should have a clear purpose.
4. Transform the Form Creation Experience
This is the most important part of Dot.Forms.
Creating forms must be effortless, interactive and enjoyable.
The editor should feel like a creative workspace rather than a technical configuration panel.
A. A genuinely intuitive visual builder
Users must be able to:

* Drag and drop fields onto their forms.
* Move fields freely between sections.
* Reorder questions and elements.
* Rearrange entire sections.
* Duplicate, copy and paste elements.
* Delete elements easily.
* Group related questions.
* Create multi-column layouts where appropriate.
* Resize supported elements.
* Move images and videos around the form.
* Undo and redo changes.
* See changes immediately.
* Preview the form at any time.
* Switch between desktop, tablet and mobile previews.

Drag-and-drop must feel smooth and precise.
Provide clear visual feedback when an element is being moved, where it can be placed and where it will appear after release.
The experience should support intuitive interactions without requiring users to understand complicated editing concepts.
Support keyboard navigation and shortcuts alongside drag-and-drop.
B. Make the editor visually enjoyable
Reconsider the entire editor layout.
Explore a design that gives users a beautiful, spacious canvas where they can see their form taking shape.
Consider:

* A central visual form canvas.
* A thoughtfully designed element library.
* Context-sensitive editing controls.
* Properties that appear when an element is selected.
* An elegant section and page navigator.
* A clear preview experience.
* Minimal distractions while editing.

Do not automatically adopt a traditional three-panel layout. Experiment with different approaches and select the one that provides the best balance of creativity, clarity and usability.
The form itself should remain the visual centre of attention.
C. Make adding elements effortless
Users should be able to discover and add form elements without navigating complicated menus.
Explore intuitive interactions such as:

* Dragging elements from a library.
* Clicking to insert an element.
* Adding a new question between existing questions.
* Inserting elements using contextual add controls.
* Duplicating an existing element.
* Quickly changing an element's type.
* Reordering elements directly on the canvas.

Use subtle transitions and immediate feedback to make these interactions feel natural.
5. Rich Media and Visual Storytelling
Forms should not be limited to questions and input fields.
Users must be able to create visually engaging experiences using images, videos and other supported media.
Images
Support:

* Image uploads.
* Image galleries.
* Image selection questions.
* Images within question descriptions.
* Full-width images.
* Side-by-side image and text layouts.
* Image backgrounds where appropriate.
* Captions and alternative text.
* Image positioning, resizing and cropping.
* Drag-and-drop image placement.

Videos
Support:

* Video uploads where technically appropriate.
* Embedded videos from authorised external providers.
* Video introductions.
* Instructional videos.
* Videos between questions.
* Video-based explanations.
* Responsive video playback.
* Video previews within the editor.

Ensure that external embeds are secure and that unsupported providers fail gracefully.
Other media and visual elements
Consider:

* Headings and rich text.
* Dividers and spacing elements.
* Icons and illustrations.
* Callout sections.
* Information blocks.
* Downloadable resources.
* Branded content sections.

Media must be treated as a first-class part of form creation, not an afterthought.
The editor should make it easy to combine questions, text, images and videos into a coherent visual experience.
6. Beautiful Forms, Not Just Beautiful Editors
The published form must be just as impressive as the builder.
Users should be able to create forms that look professionally designed without needing design skills.
Provide:

* Beautiful typography.
* Custom colours and brand themes.
* Background images and colours.
* Custom logos and brand elements.
* Flexible section layouts.
* Image and video placement.
* Consistent spacing and alignment.
* Elegant input fields.
* Thoughtful focus and hover states.
* Responsive layouts.
* Customisable buttons.
* Progress indicators.
* Beautiful success and completion screens.

Support different form experiences:

1. Classic forms: Clean, structured and familiar.
2. Conversational forms: One question at a time, with engaging transitions.
3. Multi-step forms: Organised into logical sections.
4. Landing-page forms: Visually rich forms designed for engagement and conversion.
5. Branded business forms: Professional forms aligned with an organisation's identity.

Do not force all forms into the same design.
The creator should have the freedom to choose an experience that suits the purpose of the form.
7. Motion, Animation and Interaction Design
Introduce carefully designed motion throughout Dot.Forms.
The experience should feel responsive, alive and polished without becoming distracting.
Explore:

* Smooth scrolling between form sections.
* Scroll-triggered visual transitions.
* Subtle entrance animations.
* Smooth question transitions.
* Animated progress indicators.
* Natural drag-and-drop movement.
* Gentle transitions when expanding editing controls.
* Smooth opening and closing of panels.
* Responsive button interactions.
* Elegant loading and saving states.
* Animated success and completion experiences.

Motion should communicate meaning and improve usability.
Avoid excessive animation, unnecessary parallax or effects that interfere with accessibility and performance.
Respect reduced-motion preferences.
The experience should remain fast and responsive, including on mobile devices.
8. Make Form Creation Feel Effortless
Reduce the number of decisions users must make before they can create something useful.
The creation journey should support:

* Starting from scratch.
* Choosing a professionally designed template.
* Using AI to generate a form.
* Duplicating an existing form.
* Importing supported existing forms or documents.
* Starting from an existing business workflow.

AI should be an optional assistant, not the centre of the experience.
It should help users create, improve and organise forms while preserving their control over the final result.
Users should be able to refine AI-generated forms visually, just like any other form.
9. Build a Powerful but Understandable Element Library
Review the current field library and identify missing functionality.
Consider supporting:

* Short text.
* Long text.
* Email.
* Phone.
* Number.
* Website.
* Date and time.
* Dropdowns.
* Single and multiple choice.
* Checkboxes.
* Ratings.
* Scales.
* File uploads.
* Signatures.
* Addresses.
* Currency.
* Percentage.
* Calculations.
* Matrix questions.
* Ranking questions.
* Image selection.
* Consent fields.
* Repeating sections.
* Hidden fields.
* Rich text and media.

Each element should have a clear purpose, intuitive configuration and consistent visual behaviour.
Advanced functionality should be accessible without overwhelming beginners.
10. Introduce Contextual Intelligence
Dot.Forms should help users create better forms without making the interface complicated.
Explore intelligent assistance that can:

* Recommend suitable question types.
* Identify unnecessary or repetitive questions.
* Improve question wording.
* Recommend logical form structures.
* Suggest missing information.
* Identify confusing questions.
* Recommend appropriate validation.
* Suggest relevant sections.
* Help improve form completion rates.

AI-generated recommendations must be explainable and editable.
Never silently change a user's form or invent business information.
11. Make the Experience Exceptional Across Devices
The editor and published forms must work beautifully across:

* Desktop.
* Laptop.
* Tablet.
* Mobile.

Mobile users must be able to complete forms comfortably.
The builder should also support meaningful mobile editing where practical, rather than simply shrinking the desktop interface.
Pay particular attention to touch interactions, drag-and-drop alternatives, responsive media and keyboard accessibility.
12. Performance, Accessibility and Reliability
Beautiful design is meaningless if the application feels slow or unreliable.
Implement and verify:

* Fast editor loading.
* Smooth interactions.
* Autosaving with clear save status.
* Draft recovery.
* Reliable drag-and-drop.
* Efficient media uploads.
* Image optimisation and responsive delivery.
* Lazy loading for appropriate media.
* Secure file storage.
* Reliable form previews.
* Error recovery.
* Protection against accidental data loss.
* Accessible keyboard navigation.
* Screen-reader-friendly labels.
* Appropriate contrast.
* Reduced-motion support.

Preserve existing functionality and ensure that visual improvements do not introduce regressions.
13. Design and Implementation Standards
Use the installed design skills and the existing Dot.Forms technology stack.
Use Laravel Boost when working with the Laravel application.
Before implementing:

* Understand the existing architecture.
* Identify reusable components.
* Review existing routes, models, database structures and services.
* Identify current working features.
* Identify incomplete or broken functionality.
* Avoid unnecessary rewrites.
* Preserve working integrations.
* Build reusable components rather than duplicating interface code.

Do not create mock functionality or decorative controls that do nothing.
Every visible control must perform its intended action.
Do not use placeholder images, fake content or simulated functionality in production.
Use real application data and actual working services.
14. Implementation Approach
Work through the transformation in deliberate phases.
Phase 1 — Design Audit and Direction

* Inspect the complete platform.
* Review installed skills.
* Identify AI-generated design patterns.
* Study competing products.
* Establish a new visual direction.
* Identify the highest-impact UX improvements.

Phase 2 — Design System

* Refine typography.
* Establish spacing and layout rules.
* Define colour usage.
* Create consistent components.
* Define interaction and animation principles.
* Establish responsive and accessibility standards.

Phase 3 — Form Builder Transformation

* Redesign the editing workspace.
* Implement intuitive drag-and-drop.
* Improve the element library.
* Implement contextual editing.
* Improve section management.
* Introduce smooth editing interactions.

Phase 4 — Rich Media and Visual Design

* Implement image and video support.
* Improve layout flexibility.
* Introduce visual themes.
* Improve published form presentation.
* Implement responsive previews.

Phase 5 — Interaction and Polish

* Implement purposeful motion.
* Improve scrolling and transitions.
* Refine loading and saving states.
* Improve mobile interactions.
* Remove remaining usability friction.

Phase 6 — Testing and Refinement

* Test complete form creation workflows.
* Test drag-and-drop across different element types.
* Test media uploads and embeds.
* Test editing, reordering and duplication.
* Test preview and publishing.
* Test mobile and desktop.
* Test accessibility.
* Test autosaving and recovery.
* Fix visual and functional regressions.

15. Acceptance Criteria
The transformation is not complete simply because the interface looks different.
The following must be demonstrated:

1. A new user can create a professional form without needing instructions.
2. Users can add, move, reorder, duplicate and remove form elements naturally.
3. Drag-and-drop feels smooth, precise and reliable.
4. Images and videos can be incorporated into forms.
5. Users can create visually distinctive forms without coding.
6. Published forms look as polished as the editor.
7. Scrolling and animations improve the experience rather than distract.
8. The interface feels cohesive, intentional and human-designed.
9. The application works across desktop, tablet and mobile.
10. Existing working functionality remains intact.
11. Every implemented control works.
12. Actual end-to-end tests demonstrate the complete experience.

16. Final Product Standard
I want you to challenge the current design rather than defend it.
Do not settle for a minor visual refresh.
Do not simply apply another template or redesign the dashboard.
Think like an exceptional product designer, UX researcher, interaction designer and frontend engineer working together to create a category-leading product.
Ask yourself throughout the process:

* Would someone genuinely enjoy using this?
* Does creating a form feel effortless?
* Can a first-time user understand what to do without assistance?
* Does the interface feel intentionally designed by humans?
* Is there unnecessary friction?
* Can users express their creativity without fighting the editor?
* Does every interaction feel smooth and natural?
* Would this experience stand confidently beside the world's best form-building products?

The ultimate goal: Dot.Forms should make people enjoy creating forms.
It should be beautiful enough to inspire creativity, simple enough for anyone to use, powerful enough for professional businesses and sophisticated enough for enterprise organisations.
Start with a critical assessment of the current application and a clear design transformation strategy. Then implement the improvements in the actual application, testing each major workflow as you go.
Do not stop at recommendations, mockups or a design proposal. Deliver a working, polished and demonstrably better Dot.Forms experience.
At the end, provide a clear report of what changed, what was implemented, what was tested, what remains incomplete and any architectural or technical limitations.
